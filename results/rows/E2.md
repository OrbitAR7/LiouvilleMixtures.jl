**Convergence.** One refit per revolution at perilune, rerun with the 7⁶ rule: the first
refit (t = 0.5 T) agrees with the 5⁶ rule (7.2292 against 7.2292 nats). It then breaks down after
t = 1.50 T, because its outermost nodes reach further and fall into the Moon.
So every volume after the first perilune refit is not converged, and the totals of 48 to 56
nats in the table are indicative only. The first step, which equals J at perilune, is robust.

**Lunar impacts.** The impact column is a Monte Carlo probability: 4000 draws from each
refitted Gaussian, flown over the next refit interval, counting those that pass below the
lunar surface (1737.4 km). The true pushforward has no such mass. † marks a schedule whose
later refits are not physical: some refitted Gaussian in it has more than 1% probability of
passing through the Moon. Its final volume should be read as indicative only.

- every T/8, exact (GH 5⁶): the Gaussian refitted at 1.375 T is the first with more than 1% lunar-impact probability; the largest is 0.173 [0.161, 0.185] at 1.375 T.
- every T/8, cubature: the Gaussian refitted at 1.250 T is the first with more than 1% lunar-impact probability; the largest is 0.146 [0.136, 0.158] at 1.375 T.
- every T/4, exact (GH 5⁶): the Gaussian refitted at 1.250 T is the first with more than 1% lunar-impact probability; the largest is 0.200 [0.188, 0.212] at 1.500 T.
- every T/4, cubature: the Gaussian refitted at 1.250 T is the first with more than 1% lunar-impact probability; the largest is 0.249 [0.236, 0.263] at 1.500 T.
- every T/2, exact (GH 5⁶): the Gaussian refitted at 1.500 T is the first with more than 1% lunar-impact probability; the largest is 0.239 [0.226, 0.252] at 1.500 T.
- every T/2, cubature: the Gaussian refitted at 1.500 T is the first with more than 1% lunar-impact probability; the largest is 0.428 [0.413, 0.443] at 1.500 T.
- at perilune, exact (GH 5⁶): the Gaussian refitted at 1.500 T is the first with more than 1% lunar-impact probability; the largest is 0.338 [0.323, 0.353] at 1.500 T.
- at perilune, cubature: the Gaussian refitted at 1.500 T is the first with more than 1% lunar-impact probability; the largest is 0.339 [0.324, 0.353] at 1.500 T.

**Breakdowns.** For some schedules, a refitted Gaussian's quadrature nodes fall into the
point-mass Moon, and integration fails. Those exact-refit curves stop at the last refit that
propagated:

- every T/8, exact (GH 5⁶): integration fails in the interval after the refit at 1.38 T.
- every T/2, exact (GH 5⁶): integration fails in the interval after the refit at 1.50 T.

*Covariance volume of a single Gaussian refitted on a schedule (coloured lines), against the

volume, while refits near apolune stay close to the exact curve. (a) Refit cadence; × marks
